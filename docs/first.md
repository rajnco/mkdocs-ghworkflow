# First Law of Motion

Sir Isaac Newton’s first law of motion states that an object remains at rest or continues to move at a constant speed in a straight line unless acted upon by an external force.

This law is also called the law of inertia. Inertia is the tendency of an object to resist any change in its state of motion. A book lying on a table stays still because the forces acting on it are balanced. A moving bicycle keeps moving forward unless friction, air resistance, or another force slows it down or turns it.

In other words, motion does not change by itself. An object will keep doing what it is doing unless a net force changes that condition. This is why a passenger in a bus keeps moving forward when the bus suddenly stops: the body resists the change in motion.

## Key Idea

A body does not change its motion by itself. It needs a force to start moving, stop moving, speed up, slow down, or change direction.

## Everyday Example

When a soccer ball is kicked, it moves because a force acts on it. If no force acted after that, the ball would keep moving in the same direction unless something like friction or a wall changed its motion.

In a car, passengers feel a push backward when the vehicle accelerates because their bodies resist the change in motion. Similarly, when the car stops suddenly, they lurch forward for the same reason.

## Relation to Kepler’s Laws

Yes, there is a strong relationship between Newton’s first law and Kepler’s laws of planetary motion. Kepler described how planets move around the Sun in ellipses, but Newton explained why they keep moving that way. According to Newton’s first law, a planet will continue moving in a straight line unless acted upon by a force. The Sun’s gravitational force continuously bends the planet’s path into an orbit.

Kepler’s first law says planets move in ellipses with the Sun at one focus. Newton’s first law explains that a moving planet does not naturally curve on its own; it keeps moving in a straight line unless a force bends its path. The force is gravity, and the result is a stable orbit. Kepler’s second and third laws describe the speed and timing of planetary motion, while Newton showed that these observations are consistent with the idea of inertia and gravitational force.

So, Kepler’s laws describe the observed motion of planets, while Newton’s first law explains the principle behind that motion: without a force, motion would be straight and uniform; gravity changes the direction of motion and keeps the planets in orbit. Together, the two ideas gave a deeper understanding of the structure of the solar system.

## Importance

This law explains why objects are hard to start or stop moving. It is one of the foundations of classical mechanics and helps us understand motion in everyday life and in space. It also provides the basis for understanding planetary orbits, spacecraft paths, and the motion of objects under gravity.
