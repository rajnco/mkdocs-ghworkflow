# Third Law of Motion

Newton’s third law of motion states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

This means that when one object applies a force on another object, the second object applies an equal force back in the opposite direction. The forces act on different objects, which is why they do not cancel each other out.

## Key Idea

If you push against a wall, the wall pushes back on you with the same force. If a rocket expels gas downward, the gas pushes the rocket upward.

The important idea is that the two forces act on different objects. The action force is on one object, while the reaction force acts on another. Because they are on different objects, they do not cancel each other out in the same way balanced forces on a single object do.

## Everyday Example

When you jump off the ground, your legs push downward on the ground, and the ground pushes you upward. This is why you leave the ground and rise into the air.

A swimmer pushing water backward moves forward because the water pushes back on the swimmer. In the same way, a bird pushes air downward and the air pushes the bird upward, helping it fly.

Cars and motorcycles also depend on this law. The tires push the road backward, and the road pushes the tires forward. Without this reaction force, the vehicle would not move along the ground.

## Relation to the Other Laws

The third law works together with the first and second laws. The first law tells us that objects resist changes in motion, the second law tells us how much motion changes when a force acts, and the third law explains that forces always occur in pairs during interactions between objects.

Without the third law, the second law would not fully explain how forces arise in real situations. Every push, pull, and thrust in nature is part of an action-reaction pair.

## Importance

This law explains how rockets launch, how walking works, and why forces always come in pairs. It is a fundamental idea in understanding motion and interaction between objects. It also helps explain motion in machines, animals, and vehicles, making it one of the most important laws in mechanics.
